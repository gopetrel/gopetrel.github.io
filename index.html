How to source from a Chinese manufacturer

1. Define what you actually need

Write down material, dimensions, tolerances, finish, volume and certification requirements before contacting anyone. Vague briefs produce vague quotes.

2. Shortlist two to three suppliers

Compare like with like: ask every supplier the same questions so the answers are comparable. Use the profiles here only as a starting point.

3. Request samples and a written quote

A sample confirms the part; a written quote confirms the price, MOQ, lead time and incoterms. Get both before paying a deposit.

4. Verify before you scale

Confirm the specific certificate, test report or factory claim that matters to your market. Company statements on a website are a starting point, not proof.

Honest note. This guide summarises what each company publishes about itself. It is not an endorsement, and it does not replace your own due diligence. Specifications, certifications, prices and availability change — always confirm them with the supplier.
