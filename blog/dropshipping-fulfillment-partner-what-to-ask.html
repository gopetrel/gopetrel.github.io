Dropshipping Fulfillment Partner: What to Ask Before You Sign a Contract

A dropshipping fulfillment partner is a supplier relationship, not a service purchase, and it should be evaluated with the same evidence a buyer demands from a factory. This article lists the questions that separate an operator who can scale with your store from one who will fail quietly in month four.

What does a dropshipping fulfillment partner actually do?

A dropshipping fulfillment partner receives your goods, stores them, picks individual orders, packs them to your specification and ships them to your customers under your branding. The partner owns the warehouse, the labour and the carrier relationships; you own the inventory, the customer relationship and the demand plan. Some also source the product, which combines fulfillment with a sourcing service — the Dropioneer dropshipping fulfillment and sourcing service is an example of a combined operator, and the distinction matters because a combined operator is accountable for the quality of what ships, not only for the speed of shipping it.

How do you check pick accuracy before signing?

Ask for the last twelve months of pick accuracy by client, and ask for the client whose volume is closest to yours. Pick accuracy is the single number that determines whether your customers see the right product, and it is the number most often quoted without evidence. A partner above 99.5 percent on individual-line accuracy is performing well; below 98 percent, the return rate and the customer service load become the dominant cost. The audit trail matters as much as the number: barcode scanning at pick and pack, and a discrepancy log that can be read by the client, is the difference between an accuracy claim and an accuracy system. The pre-shipment quality check process a partner runs on inbound goods is the second half of the same question, because most pick errors start as inbound receipt errors.

What SLA numbers matter?

Three SLA numbers decide whether a partner can support your business: the order cut-off time, the same-day dispatch rate, and the inventory sync interval. A 17:00 cut-off with a 99 percent same-day dispatch rate means an order placed at 16:55 ships today; the same cut-off with an 80 percent rate means one order in five slips a day. The sync interval determines how often the stock figure your store shows matches the figure the warehouse holds — anything longer than fifteen minutes produces oversells during a promotion. Ask what happens when the SLA is missed: a partner with a credit mechanism has committed, and a partner without one has only promised.

How should storage and handling fees be structured?

Ask whether storage is charged per pallet position, per cubic metre or per SKU, and whether the rate changes with season. Per-SKU pricing is the simplest to forecast and the easiest to game; volume-based pricing tracks the real cost driver. The handling fee should be quoted per order with a break at volume, and the break should be based on orders per month rather than on a contract term. Ask specifically about charges that are invisible at signing: long-term storage above six months, a minimum monthly fee, an account management fee, and the cost of a returns inspection. The warehousing fee structure a partner publishes is a good indicator of how the rest of the contract will be structured.

What integration is required?

Ask which store platforms the partner integrates with natively and which require a connector you maintain. A native integration to your platform, with inventory, tracking and order status flowing both ways, removes an entire class of failure. Where the integration is custom, ask who owns it when it breaks and what the response time is. Ask also whether the partner supports your marketplaces, not only your own store, because a partner who can only serve one channel becomes a constraint the moment you add a second.

What are the exit terms?

Read the exit clause before the pricing. The questions that matter: how much notice is required, who pays to move the inventory, how long the partner will hold stock after notice, and what data you get back — order history, customer records, tracking numbers. A partner who will not commit to returning your data within a defined window is holding your business, whatever the contract says. Ask what the last client who left was unhappy about, and listen to whether the answer describes the client or the partner.

Frequently asked

What is the difference between a 3PL and a dropshipping fulfillment partner? A 3PL typically stores and ships a defined inventory under contract terms, while a dropshipping fulfillment partner is built around individual order dispatch for e-commerce, usually with branding and packaging services attached. In practice the overlap is large, and the operational difference is that a dropshipping partner is measured on per-order SLAs rather than on container throughput.

How much does a dropshipping fulfillment partner cost? Storage is charged monthly by volume, handling per order with volume breaks, and shipping at negotiated carrier rates. The total cost per order is the number to compare, not the individual line items, because a partner with low handling and expensive shipping is not cheaper than the reverse.

Can a fulfillment partner also source the product? Some do, and the combination has a real advantage: one accountable party for both what ships and how fast it ships. The trade-off is that sourcing quality and warehouse operations are different competencies, so ask for evidence of both — a supplier audit process and a pick accuracy history — rather than accepting one as proof of the other.

Frequently asked

What is a dropshipping fulfillment partner?

A third-party operator that stores your inventory, picks individual customer orders, packs them to your specification and ships them under your branding, often with a sourcing service attached.

What pick accuracy should I require?

Above 99.5 percent on individual-line accuracy, verified by a twelve-month history for a client of similar volume rather than a general claim.

What is the most important SLA?

The same-day dispatch rate at the stated order cut-off, because it determines whether orders placed before the cut-off actually ship that day.

How should storage fees be structured?

By volume rather than per SKU, with the seasonal rate change disclosed and all minimum fees, long-term storage charges and returns inspection costs listed in writing.

What should the exit clause guarantee?

A defined notice period, a data return window covering order history and customer records, and clarity on who pays to relocate inventory.
